1. Welcome

Muhasabah Mirror helps you look honestly at how you lived this week, then compare it with how the scholars of Ahlus Sunnah handled the same kinds of affairs.

“Take account of yourselves before you are taken to account.”'Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah be pleased with him, reported by at-Tirmidhi

Muhasabah means self-accounting. The app turns it into a habit you can keep: you write down one real event, rate how you acted on five simple measures, then hold the event up beside a scholar's teaching or reported conduct and rate yourself on three practices that scholar modeled.

Over time the dashboard shows where your creed is reaching your conduct and your commerce, and where it is not. It is built to help you resist two quiet pulls: hours lost to harmful social media, and the drift into destructive materialism.

Who it is for

Any believer who wants to reflect on real life: students, parents, employees, business owners and leaders. The Creed-to-Commerce™ lens is especially useful if your income, your spending and your faith all need to answer to the same standard.

What it is not

  • It is not a fatwa service and does not give religious rulings. Ask a qualified teacher for those.
  • It does not judge your standing with Allah. Only He knows the hearts. The scores show direction over time.
  • It does not quote the scholars. The lenses are short summaries in plain words. Go to the original books for their exact language.

2. Quick start

Your first reflection takes about five minutes.

  1. Open the app. It starts with eight example entries so you can see how everything looks. They are marked Example.
  2. Clear the examples when you are ready. Go to Reports & Data and press Clear example entries. Your own entries are never touched.
  3. Press New. The green-and-gold New button sits in the bar at the bottom of every screen.
  4. Describe one real event. Choose the area of life, give it a short title, and write what happened and how you responded.
  5. Slide your five ratings. Rate sincerity, restraint, gratitude, benefit to others and presence from 1 to 10.
  6. Measure yourself against a scholar. Read the lens, then answer the three practices: Not yet, Sometimes or Consistently.
  7. Write one next step you can finish this week, then press Save reflection.
  8. Open the Dashboard to see your score, radar chart and trend update.
Start smallOne honest entry a day for a week is worth more than one long entry you never repeat.

3. A tour of the app

Five screens, plus an edit dock that stays at the bottom of all of them. Use the tabs under the green header to move between screens.

Dashboard

The Dashboard answers one question: where do I stand? Use the buttons at the top right to choose 7 days, 30 days, 90 days or All.

  • Summary strip. Number of reflections, your average behavior score out of 100, your average mirror alignment, and your day streak (consecutive days with an entry, counting from today or yesterday).
  • Twelve areas of life. A radar chart of your average score in each area. The dashed gold ring is your target. An area with no reflections shows at the centre with its label dimmed.
  • Five measures of behavior. Average rating out of 10 for sincerity, restraint, gratitude, benefit and presence. The gold tick is your target.
  • Creed to Commerce™. Average score for each layer: Creed, Character, Conduct and Commerce.
  • Behavior score over time. Each point is one reflection. The gold dot is your latest. Hover a point to see its title.
  • Distraction check. Minutes on screens that you logged, by week, against your daily goal (shown as a weekly dashed line).
  • Materialism check. Unplanned spending you logged, by week.
  • Area to strengthen. Your lowest-scoring area in the period, with a scholar's lens, a question to ask yourself, and shortcuts to reflect on it or read more.

Reflect

This is where you write an entry. It has four parts, top to bottom.

Part 1. The event

  • Date. Today by default. Change it to log something from an earlier day.
  • Area of life. Choose one of the twelve areas. The Creed-to-Commerce™ layer fills in automatically and you can change it.
  • Short title, what happened, what you felt. Write facts first, then the feeling, then the choice you made.

Part 2. Measure your behavior

Move each slider from 1 (far from the ideal) to 10 (close to it). The live score under the sliders updates as you move them. Then add two optional numbers for this event: minutes spent on social media or screens, and any unplanned spending. These feed the weekly checks on the Dashboard.

Part 3. Hold it up to the scholars

Pick a scholar from the list. The app shows a verse or hadith for the area, that scholar's lens, and three practices to rate yourself on. Choose Not yet, Sometimes or Consistently for each. Below them is a question to ask yourself.

Part 4. One step forward

Write one small, specific action you can finish this week. Press Save reflection. Use Clear form to start over.

Journal

Every saved reflection appears here as a card, with its date, area, layer, score and a mini-bar for each of the five measures.

  • Find entries. Search by words, filter by area or layer, and sort by newest, oldest, lowest score or highest score.
  • Edit. Opens the entry in the Reflect screen with a gold banner. Change anything, then Save changes, or press Cancel editing.
  • Duplicate. Copies the entry with today's date and “(copy)” added to the title. Useful for recurring situations.
  • PDF. Downloads a PDF of that single reflection.
  • Delete. Press once and the button turns red and says Confirm delete. Press again within four seconds to delete. A toast lets you undo it.

The coloured left edge of each card shows its band: green for Steady, gold for Growing, red for Needs muhasabah. The label always appears in words as well.

Scholar Mirror

The Mirror is the library behind the measuring. Use it any time you want to read, not log.

  • By area of life. Pick one of the twelve areas to see its verse or hadith, each scholar's lens, the three practices, and a question to ask yourself.
  • By scholar. Pick a scholar to see his era, his works, a one-line note, and how he speaks to every area where he has a lens.
  • Each page has a Reflect on… button that opens a new entry already set to that area.

Reports and data

  • PDF report. Choose a period (7, 30, 90 days or all), choose full entries or scores only, and decide whether to include example entries. Press Download PDF report. See Chapter 8 for what the PDF contains.
  • Download CSV. A spreadsheet of the same entries.
  • Backup and import. Download backup (JSON) saves everything. Import backup adds entries from a backup file.
  • Clear example entries and Erase everything. Erase asks you to press twice.
  • Your targets. Set your behavior target (default 8 out of 10), your daily screen goal (default 60 minutes) and your currency symbol.

The edit dock

The dark green bar at the bottom of every screen holds the tools you will use most, so you never have to hunt for them.

ButtonWhat it does

4. How measuring works

Two scores come from every entry: a behavior score from your five ratings, and a mirror alignment from your three scholar practices.

The five measures

Rate what you did in the event, not who you are. Be strict with yourself and fair to yourself. Use this guide to keep your ratings consistent from week to week.

MeasureThe question1 to 34 to 67 to 89 to 10
Sincerity (Ikhlas)Was it for Allah, not for show or praise?Mostly for image or rewardMixed motivesMostly for Allah, with some self-regardWould be the same if no one ever knew
Restraint (Sabr)Did I hold back from what I should not say or do?Gave in to the urgeHeld back late or partlyHeld back with effortCalm and in control throughout
Gratitude (Shukr)Was my heart content with what Allah gave me?Complaint or envy ledGrateful in words, restless insideMostly contentContent, and showed it in action
Benefit (Nafa')Did other people gain from how I acted?Others lost outNo real effect on othersSomeone clearly gainedLasting good for people
Presence (Hudur)Was I present, free of distraction?Mind elsewhere, screen in handDistracted in partMostly presentFully present

The behavior score

Behavior score = average of the five ratings × 10. So five ratings of 7, 8, 7, 9 and 8 average 7.8 and score 78.

ScoreBandMeaning
80 to 100SteadyYou acted close to the ideal. Notice what helped and repeat it.
60 to 79GrowingGood effort with clear room to improve.
0 to 59Needs muhasabahThis event deserves a closer look and a specific next step.

Mirror alignment

For each of the three practices you choose Not yet (0), Sometimes (1) or Consistently (2). The alignment is the total out of 6, shown as a percentage. For example, ratings of 2, 1 and 1 total 4 out of 6, which is 67%. If you leave any practice unanswered, that entry has no alignment figure and is left out of the average.

The Creed-to-Commerce™ layers

Every reflection sits in one layer, so you can see how far your convictions travel.

Creed

Belief and intention. Prayer and sincerity start here.

Character

Akhlaq. Speech, patience, humility and how you treat the community.

Conduct

Daily practice. Time, screens, family and the pursuit of knowledge.

Commerce

Earning and spending. Halal earning, business and your relationship with wealth.

A healthy pattern is a Commerce layer that scores close to your Creed layer. A wide gap between them is a sign that your faith is not yet reaching your wallet and your work.

Targets

Your behavior target, set on Reports & Data, appears as a dashed gold ring and tick on the charts. Pick a target that stretches you but that you can honestly reach. The default is 8 out of 10 (80 out of 100).

6. A worked example

Here is how one of the built-in example entries is measured, step by step.

Two hours lost scrolling after Isha

Area: Social Media & Screens · Layer: Conduct · Scholar: Ibn al-Qayyim · Screens: 130 minutes

“I picked up my phone to check one message and was still scrolling at midnight, comparing my business to other people's posts. Restless and a little envious. I told myself it was research.”

  1. Rate the five measures. Sincerity 5, restraint 3, gratitude 5, benefit 2, presence 2. The average is 3.4, so the behavior score is 34, in the Needs muhasabah band.
  2. Read the lens. Ibn al-Qayyim traces how a passing thought grows into a desire, then a resolve, then an act, and teaches guarding the heart at the first stage. A feed is a stream of first-stage thoughts.
  3. Rate the three practices. Set a limit and kept to it: Not yet (0). Muted what stirs envy or comparison: Sometimes (1). Replaced scrolling with something beneficial: Not yet (0). The total is 1 out of 6, so the mirror alignment is 17%.
  4. Log the screen minutes. 130 goes into the weekly Distraction check.
  5. Write the step. “Charge the phone outside the bedroom and set a 45-minute daily limit.” Specific, small and doable this week.

Compare it with another example: Declined a rushed sale that hid a defect (Halal Earning & Business, with Ibn Taymiyyah). Ratings of 9, 8, 7, 8 and 8 average 8.0, a score of 80, which is Steady. Practices of 2, 2 and 1 total 5 out of 6, an alignment of 83%. The goal is not a perfect score. It is to notice the pattern and move it.

7. A weekly routine

A simple rhythm that keeps muhasabah realistic.

Daily · 5 minutes

After Isha, or at another quiet time, pick the one event of the day with the clearest lesson. Log it honestly. End with a single next step.

Weekly · 20 minutes

On Friday, open the Dashboard on 7 days. Read your Area to strengthen. Check the screen and spending bars. Choose one area to focus on next week.

Monthly · 30 minutes

Set the Dashboard to 30 days. Compare Creed with Commerce. Download the PDF report. Share it with a trusted teacher or mentor if you wish. Download a backup.

At a turning point

Before Ramadan, at the new year or after a business decision, read the Scholar Mirror for the relevant area and write a longer reflection.

Guarding against distraction and materialism

  • Log the screen minutes even when they are embarrassing. An honest number is more useful than a flattering one.
  • Set a daily screen goal on Reports & Data and watch the dashed line on the weekly chart.
  • Log unplanned spending as it happens. Ask the question from the Wealth lens: what need was I trying to fill?
  • Use the Duplicate button for patterns that keep returning, then edit the ratings. Seeing the same situation improve is motivating.
  • Pair every low score with one concrete step, not a general promise.
Keep it kindMuhasabah is accountability with hope. A low score is information for tomorrow, not a reason to stop writing.

8. Your data and privacy

Your journal stays on your own device unless you choose to export it.

Where entries are stored

Entries and settings are saved in your web browser, on the device you use. There is no account and no server holding your journal, so no one at BMPA can see what you write.

ImportantBecause storage is in the browser, your journal can disappear if you clear site data, use a private window, switch browsers or devices, or the app moves to a new web address. Download a backup regularly.

The PDF report

The report is built on your device. It contains a cover with the period and date, a summary strip, area and layer bars with your target, the five measures, the distraction and spending totals, strengths and a focus area with a scholar's lens, every entry in the period (with narrative, scholar practices and next steps if you chose Full), a short note on method, and a footer with page numbers and the Creed-to-Commerce™ line.

  • The PDF button on a journal card makes a report for one reflection only.
  • The PDF shows the Basmala in English, since the PDF tool cannot draw Arabic script. Arabic text in your own notes will not appear in the PDF. It stays in the app.
  • The PDF tool loads from the internet, so you need a connection the first time.

Backup and moving to another device

  1. On the old device, press Download backup (JSON) on Reports & Data.
  2. On the new device, open the app and press Import backup.
  3. Choose the file. Entries are added to what is already there. Entries that already exist are skipped, so importing twice is safe. Your targets are also restored.

CSV export

Press Download CSV to open your journal in Excel or Google Sheets. Columns include date, area, layer, title, narrative, scholar, the five ratings, behavior score, mirror percentage, screen minutes, spending and next step. Use this file for your own analysis. It is not a restore file. Use the JSON backup for that.

Sharing a computer

If other people use the same browser, they can open your journal. Download a backup, then use Erase everything before you leave.

9. Deploying with GitHub and Vercel

For BMPA staff or anyone hosting the app. Both the app and this manual are single files, so no build step is needed.

Files

muhasabah-mirror/
  index.html     (the app)
  manual.html    (this manual)

Steps

  1. Create a GitHub repository, for example muhasabah-mirror.
  2. Add both files at the top level using Add file → Upload files, then commit to the main branch.
  3. Open Vercel and choose Add New → Project. Import the repository.
  4. Leave the settings alone. Framework preset Other, no build command, no output directory. Press Deploy.
  5. Add a custom domain under Settings → Domains if you want one.
  6. Update later by committing a new index.html. Vercel redeploys automatically.

The app has a User manual link in its tab bar that points to manual.html, and the manual's Open the app button points back to index.html, so they work together when stored side by side.

Keep the web address stableEach address has its own browser storage. If you change the domain, users will see an empty journal until they import a backup. Tell users to back up before any move.
Online resourcesThe app loads its fonts from Google Fonts and its PDF tool from cdnjs. If either is blocked, the app still works but uses fallback fonts, or cannot make PDFs. Hosting those files yourself removes the dependency.

10. Questions and fixes

My PDF will not download.

Check that your browser has not blocked downloads from the site. Check your internet connection, because the PDF tool loads online. If you chose a period with no reflections, the app will say there is nothing to include. Tick Include example entries or add a reflection.

My journal is empty after I reopened the app.

The browser probably cleared site data, or you are in a private window, a different browser or at a new web address. Import your latest backup. If you have none, the entries cannot be recovered.

Can I edit or delete the example entries?

Yes. Edit or delete them like any other. Once you save an edited example, it becomes your own entry and loses the Example label. Clear example entries removes every remaining example at once.

I deleted something by mistake.

Press Undo in the dock, or in the message that appears after deleting. Undo works until you reload the page.

Why does the dashboard show a dash instead of a number?

There are no reflections in the selected period, or none with all three scholar practices answered. Pick a longer period or add an entry.

An area on the radar chart sits in the centre.

It has no reflections yet in this period. Its label is dimmed. Reflect on it from the Area to strengthen panel or from the Scholar Mirror.

How is the day streak counted?

It counts consecutive days that each have at least one entry, ending today. If you have not yet written today, it counts back from yesterday so your streak is not lost mid-day.

I log events from earlier days. Does that work?

Yes. Change the date in Part 1. The charts and weekly bars use the date you set.

Can I change the currency?

Yes. Enter any symbol on Reports & Data under Your targets. It applies to the weekly chart, the journal cards and the PDF.

Can two people share one journal?

Not directly. Each browser has its own journal. A couple or a study circle can compare PDF reports or exchange backup files.

12. Notes and cautions

  • Not a fatwa. Muhasabah Mirror is a reflection aid. For rulings on worship, finance or family matters, consult a qualified scholar.
  • Not a diagnosis. The app is not mental health care. If you feel overwhelmed or in distress, speak to a trusted person or a qualified professional.
  • Summaries, not quotations. The scholar lenses paraphrase well-known teaching and reported conduct. Verify against the original works before you teach or cite them.
  • Your ratings are your own. The scores are only as honest as your answers. They measure direction, not worth.
